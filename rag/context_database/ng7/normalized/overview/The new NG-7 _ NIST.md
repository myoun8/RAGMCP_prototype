# The new NG-7

> DEPRECATION NOTICE: This page is no longer being updated and the information may be out of date.

NG-7 currently provides neutrons to a 30 m SANS instrument, a test station (PHADES) with a monochromatic beam ($\lambda = 4.1$ Å), two neutron interferometers, and a horizontal sample reflectometer. The guide is straight (Be and Bi filters remove unwanted radiation before the SANS machine). 

The new guide will also be straight and the same size (50 mm by 120 mm) as the current NG-7. However, it will be coated with $m = 2$ supermirror rather than $^{58}\text{Ni}$. This change will provide a significant gain for the instruments on NG-7.

## Instrument Changes and Relocations

The NCNR will retire the horizontal sample reflectometer (approximately 30 years old) and relocate the $\alpha$-$\gamma$ instrument from NG-6 to NG-7. This move facilitates the upgrade of the cold neutron imaging station on NG-6. 

While the old horizontal reflectometer will be removed, reflectometry experiments on horizontal surfaces can still be performed using MAGIk. MAGIk is considered superior to the previous reflectometer on NG-7 for two reasons:
1. The data rate is better.
2. The sample remains stationary throughout the experiment (utilizing the large height of NG-D to move the beam).

## The $\alpha$-$\gamma$ Instrument

Moving the $\alpha$-$\gamma$ instrument to NG-7 will increase the data rate nearly 3x and provide more physical space, significantly improving operational flexibility.

The device relies on a totally absorbing neutron detector and has been used to measure the absolute neutron fluence of a cold, monoenergetic neutron beam to a precision five times better than the previous state of the art. In conjunction with a weakly absorbing neutron detector known as the flux monitor, it is used for:
* Beam-based measurements of the neutron lifetime.
* Precise measurements of key neutron absorption cross sections (notably in Li, B, and $^{235}\text{U}(n,f)$).
* Recalibrating the US national radioactive neutron source NBS-1.

### Alpha-Gamma 2.0

A design for a new pair of instruments (version 2.0) is underway to meet the continuing need for neutron metrology with even smaller uncertainties. Key features of version 2.0 include:
* **Larger Size:** Accommodates a larger neutron beam.
* **Enhanced Detection:** A larger number of gamma-ray and solid-state detectors to increase solid angle and reduce geometry-based systematic errors.
* **Operational Improvements:** Simplified features to render the routine operation of these delicate devices safer.

The ultimate goal is to operate these devices as a service for interested outside users.
