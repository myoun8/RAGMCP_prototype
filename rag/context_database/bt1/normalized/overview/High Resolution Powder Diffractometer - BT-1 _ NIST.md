# High Resolution Powder Diffractometer - BT-1

The BT-1 high-resolution neutron powder diffractometer is used to obtain neutron powder diffraction data for crystallographic analysis by the Rietveld method or other characterization purposes. It is a 32 detector instrument that can be used with three different monochromators and two different incident Soller collimators, allowing the instrument response to be tailored to the needs of the experiment. 

The instrument can be used with furnaces, refrigerators and cryostats so that data may be collected at temperatures from 0.3 to 2000 K, and with magnetic fields. For room-temperature data collection, a six-position sample changer is available.

The BT-1 instrument may be used for academic and proprietary measurements. Requests or Mail-in Proposals are made through the IMS account. While there is no fee associated with use of BT-1 for work that will be published in the open literature, Federal regulations require cost-recovery for proprietary work.

## Specifications/Capabilities

### Choice of Monochromator and Collimator

The choice of three focusing monochromators—Ge(311), Cu(311), and Ge(733)—and either a 60', 15' or a 7' in-pile Soller collimator allows optimization of the instrument's resolution minimum versus relative diffraction intensities. All monochromators provide data up to 165 degrees in 2$\theta$ range.

| Monochromator | Wavelength ($\text{\AA}$) | Takeoff Angle | Characteristics | Typical Collection Time |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ge(311)** | 2.079 | 75° | Highest neutron intensity; best resolution at low scattering angles. Intensities $\sim$3x Cu(311). Useful for zeolites, magnetic ordering, and phase changes. | 15 mins to few hours |
| **Cu(311)** | 1.540 | 90° | Optimal balance between intensity and resolution; perfect Gaussian line shape. Used for Rietveld refinement of superconductors, fullerenes, ceramics, and intermetallics. | 1 to 12 hours (avg 3-4h) |
| **Ge(733)** | 1.197 | 120° | Best resolution at high scattering angles. Intensities $\sim$25% of Cu(311). Used for perovskites (subtle symmetry lowering) or precise oxygen vacancy determination. | 4 to 24 hours |

**Note on Ge(733) Resolution:** With 7' collimation, Gaussian line shapes with widths (FWHM) as small as 10' ($\Delta d/d = 8 \times 10^{-4}$) are observed, though intrinsic sample broadening often exceeds this. Use is restricted to applications where highest resolution is critical and sample quality/quantity is sufficient.

### Mail-in Sample Service
The mail-in service is intended for users with basic needs: data collection at room temperature or within the range of a closed-cycle fridge (5-300 K). Approximately 25% of beamtime per cycle is allocated to mail-in samples.

#### Workflow for Mail-in Service
1. **Proposal:** Submit a proposal via the NIST IMS system.
2. **Configuration:** Select the correct sample environment:
   - `'none'` for room temperature.
   - `'CCR'` for 5K-300K.
3. **Request:** Explicitly indicate the request for "mail-in service" in the proposal.
4. **Shipping:** Once accepted, mail samples to the provided address.
5. **Preparation:** The crystallography team prepares samples for analysis.
6. **Coordination:** After allocation, instrument scientists will discuss requirements and provide advice on optimal conditions.

#### Pros and Cons of Mail-in Service
**Advantages:**
- No travel to NCNR required.
- No need to monitor the instrument in real-time.
- Online data viewing available.
- Access to instrument scientist expertise for experiment design.
- Data is examined by scientists and converted to appropriate formats (standard is `.gsas` files for refinement).
- Sample storage available at NCNR or return shipping upon request (requires at least one week for safety/activity measurements).

**Disadvantages:**
- User cannot make real-time adjustments during the run. If unexpected behavior occurs, the instrument scientist may not be able to adjust the experiment exactly as the user would prefer.

## Proprietary Research using BT-1

BT-1 is available for proprietary research on a cost-recovery basis.
- **Cost:** Generally approximately $3,000 per 24-hour period, depending on the level of NIST personnel involvement.
- **Non-Proprietary Work:** No charge for industrial or academic users if results are published in open literature in a timely fashion.
