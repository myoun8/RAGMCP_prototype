# BT-8 Diffractometer for Stress and Texture

The BT-8 diffractometer supports primarily the engineering research needs of users from federal agencies, academia, and the American industry. The main areas of research are residual stresses in structural materials, the measurement of applied stresses during multiaxial straining, and preferred orientation and phase composition as a result of materials processing. Materials of interest include high-strength alloys used in additive manufacturing, steels for naval and automotive applications, and weld-induced stresses in structural materials.

Stresses are determined from the measurement of elastic changes of interatomic lattice spacings at depths up to several centimeters, with spatial resolutions on the order of $\approx 1$ millimeter. Spatial resolution is determined by the dimensions of the incident and diffracted neutron beams, both of which are set through apertures or slits. Lattice spacings change linearly with stress, which is both directional and generally dependent on the location in a specimen. Therefore, the determination of tri-axial stresses for a given location in the sample requires different orientations of the sample.

## Instrument Features

The upgrade of all BT8 core components has been completed. The instrument now features:
* A large, high-pressure, 10 bar $^3\text{He}$ area detector.
* Single and multi-wavelength Si-crystal monochromators with variable curvature.
* Two multi-axial high-capacity straining devices.
* Separate sample stages for small and large samples.
* A digital image correlation (DIC) system for the measurement of plastic strain during multiaxial loading.
* A phi-chi goniometer with XYZ table serving as both a sample positioning stage and a sample changer for high-throughput texture measurements.
* A coordinate measurement probe for aligning samples with complicated or irregular shapes.

The modular instrument design allows for flexibility regarding measurable diffraction peaks, wavelengths, diffraction angles, gauge volumes, and specimen size. Gauge volumes can be defined through primary/secondary apertures (single peak measurement) or using conical slits for measurements utilizing multiple peaks. 

* **Large Samples:** Samples up to $\approx 0.6\text{ m}$ and heavy weights are positioned and oriented using the base goniometer, with a load-balancing instrument jib crane to reduce the weigh-on-table.
* **Small Specimens:** Specimens $< 300\text{ mm}$ can be positioned and oriented without re-mounting using a Eulerian cradle with an integrated XYZ positioning table. The Euler cradle, combined with a sample changer, enables fast measurement of complete pole figures and phase composition.

## Sample Environment

Sample environment options focus on lattice strain measurements under applied stress in both uniaxial and multi-axial deformation. 

* **Uniaxial Loads:** Mechanically actuated load frames allow loads up to $100\text{ kN}$.
* **Multi-axial Deformations:** 
    * **Shear Device:** Allows straining in various modes, including simple and pure shear, up to $30\text{ kN}$ of applied force.
    * **Octo-strain Eight Arm Device:** Designed for complex strain paths in the deformation of high-strength steels via eight individually controlled actuators.
* **Stress Axis Determination:** Both multi-axial devices can be rotated to enable the determination of the principal stress axes.
* **Plastic Strain Measurement:** Because spatial distribution of plastic strains and stresses is inhomogeneous, a digital image correlation (DIC) system is used to determine plastic strains at the specific locations of the neutron measurement.

The combination of plastic strain data (DIC) and true stresses (neutron diffraction) produces the stress-strain relationships necessary for predicting material behavior in sheet metal forming.
