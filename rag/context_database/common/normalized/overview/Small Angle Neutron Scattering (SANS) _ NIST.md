# Small Angle Neutron Scattering (SANS)

Small-Angle Neutron Scattering (SANS) probes material structure on the nanometer ($10^{-9}$ m) to micrometer ($10^{-6}$ m) scale. Structures on this length scale are critical to the performance of advanced engineering materials. Examples include:

*   **High impact plastics:** Toughness depends on the admixture of stiff and flexible segments of polymer molecules.
*   **Biological processes:** Crucial structures within cells.
*   **Information storage:** Magnetic disks.
*   **Metallurgy:** Hardness of steels and superalloys.
*   **Superconductors:** Conduction of current.

## Technical Principles

The SANS instruments at the NCNR utilize cold (long wavelength) neutrons and tight beam collimation to probe length scales ($d$) ranging from 1 nm to nearly 10,000 nm. The length scale probed is determined by the relationship between the neutron wavelength ($\lambda$) and the scattering angle ($\theta$):

$$d \approx \frac{\lambda}{\theta}$$

## SANS Instruments
The following instruments are available for SANS:

*   BT5 USANS
*   nSoft 10m SANS
*   NG7 30m SANS
