# CHRNS MACS - The Multi-Axis Crystal Spectrometer

The Multi-Axis Crystal Spectrometer (MACS) at the NIST Center for Neutron Research is a third generation cold neutron spectrometer that provides ultra high sensitivity access to dynamic correlations in condensed matter on length scales from 0.1 nm to 50 nm and energy scales from 2.35 meV to 16 meV. The project is funded jointly by the NIST Center for Neutron Research, the National Science Foundation, and the Johns Hopkins University.

## Specifications/Capabilities

The Multi-Axis Crystal Spectrometer takes advantage of the large solid angle view of the cold source to deliver a high intensity neutron beam at sample position. 

### Key Technical Features
*   **Monochromator:** A large doubly focusing PG monochromator can deliver up to $5 \times 10^8$ neutrons/cm²/s in a 2 cm x 4 cm area at sample position.
*   **Neutron Guide:** A focusing super-mirror guide between the monochromator and sample enhances the flux on sample by approximately 20%.
*   **Beam Control:** 
    *   A variable beam aperture upstream of the monochromator controls the overall angular distribution of incident neutrons.
    *   Radial collimators control the energy resolution.
    *   Q and E resolution can be controlled virtually independently through these remote controlled devices.
*   **Detection System:** 
    *   Twenty independent monochromatic channels with variable final energy.
    *   An energy integrating detector in each channel.
    *   Simultaneous operation provides 0.15 Sr monochromatic detection plus 0.15 Sr diffraction detection, offering more than an order of magnitude gain in detection efficiency for surveys compared to a conventional TAS.

### Actual MACS Configuration

| Parameter | Specification |
| :--- | :--- |
| Incident energy | 2.3 to 17 meV |
| Energy resolution | 0.05 to 1.4 meV (FWHM) |
| Monochromator | Doubly focusing PG002 |
| Analyzer | 20 analyzers vertically focusing PG(002) providing 0.15 Sr of detection. Cooled Be, BeO and HOPG filters and 90' collimators before the analyzers. |
| Detector | 40 He detectors (20 spectroscopic and 20 diffraction detectors) |
| Flux at sample | $5 \times 10^8$ neutrons/cm²-s at 12meV open collimator |

## Scientific Opportunities/Applications

### Magnetic Materials and Quantum States
*   **Local Magnetic Anisotropy:** Development of theoretical frameworks to model local magnetic anisotropy in strongly correlated materials, such as the antiferromagnetic $\text{CeRhIn}_5$.
*   **Time-Resolved Inelastic Neutron Scattering (TRINS):** Extension of spectroscopy to probe time-dependent nonequilibrium dynamics in magnetic materials (beyond $10\ \mu\text{s}$), such as singlet ground state molecular magnets induced by pulsed microwaves.
*   **Weyl Semimetals:** Investigation of Weyl-mediated helical magnetism in $\text{NdAlSi}$, linking long-wavelength helical magnetic order to nesting vectors between non-trivial Fermi pockets.
*   **Spin Ice:** Use of time-resolved elastic neutron scattering to investigate monopolar and dipolar relaxation processes in the spin ice material $\text{Ho}_2\text{Ti}_2\text{O}_7$.
*   **Quantum Spin Liquids:** 
    *   Observation of a quantum spin liquid in calcium-chromium oxide.
    *   Discovery of a "spin-orbital liquid" state.
    *   Confirmation of spin liquid states in kagome-lattice antiferromagnets.

### Other Applications
*   **Piezoelectrics:** Research into the mechanism of piezoelectricity in relaxors (e.g., PMN) using neutron scattering to clarify the role of random electric fields.
