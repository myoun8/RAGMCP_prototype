# Imaging: Advanced Neutron Imaging Facility

The NIST Neutron Imaging Facility (NNIF) at the NIST Center for Neutron Research (NCNR) has been available as a national user facility since 2006. It provides a "plug and play" approach to imaging operating fuel cells and electrolyzers. 

In 2016, the NIST-NeXT system was installed, enabling simultaneous neutron/X-ray tomography of complex materials and systems, including:
* Batteries
* Concrete
* Shales
* Two-phase flow in granular media

## Facility Neutron Optics

The facility is located at Beam Tube 2 (BT-2) at the NCNR. The NCNR provides an intense source of thermal neutrons collimated using a tapered plug (pinhole optics), resulting in a beam that is nearly uniform in intensity across the diameter at the image plane.

### Radiation Filtering
To protect electronic equipment and personnel from high-energy neutrons and gamma rays, a filter is placed downstream of the tapered collimator. This filter consists of 10 cm of bismuth single crystal cooled to liquid nitrogen (LN) temperatures (77 K). Cooling the crystal reduces vibrational phonon modes, making the crystal nearly transparent (~60% transmission) to thermal neutrons while remaining opaque to high-energy neutrons and gamma rays.

### Collimation
The filtered beam is collimated via a thermal neutron pinhole located downstream of the bismuth filter. The system supports various aperture sizes to adjust the $L/d$ ratio and image resolution.

## System Components and Layout

1.  **Rotating Drum:** Located after the pinhole, this drum has four positions:
    *   Three positions for additional collimation (1 cm, 2 cm, and open).
    *   One position to block the beam when not in use.
2.  **Shielding:** A steel-encased mixture of wax (to thermalize high-energy neutrons) and steel shot (to absorb gammas) prevents radiation hazards.
3.  **Flight Tube:** A sealed, evacuated aluminum tube that transports the collimated beam to the sample position.
4.  **Sample Position:** An open-to-air area featuring a rotating and translating stage for the object being radiographed.
5.  **Imaging Systems:**
    *   **Neutron Camera:** Digitizes the 2D neutron distribution.
    *   **X-ray Imaging System:** Positioned perpendicular to the neutron beam to enable simultaneous neutron and X-ray tomography.
6.  **Beam Stop:** A safety component designed to stop the unattenuated beam, including remaining high-energy neutrons and gamma rays.
7.  **Infrastructure:** Includes specialized equipment for fuel cell testing.
